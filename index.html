Physiology: Normal Human Body (NHB)

Comprehensive overview of Homeostasis and Body Fluids.

1. Homeostasis

Definition & Importance

  • Definition: The maintenance of a constant internal environment in response to changes in external or internal conditions.
  • Importance: Essential for the survival of every cell. It keeps the internal environment within physiological limits. Failure leads to disease, dysfunction, or death.
  • All body systems share in maintaining homeostasis (e.g., Respiratory system regulates O2/CO2, Kidneys regulate water/electrolytes).

Homeostatic Control System Components

  • Sensor (Receptor): Detects the change or imbalance.
  • Control Center: Receives information and determines the appropriate response (e.g., the Brain).
  • Transmission: Sends impulses via electrical (nerves) or chemical (hormones) pathways.
  • Effector: Executes the response to correct the deviation.

Feedback Mechanisms

  • Negative Feedback: The end product reduces or inhibits the original stimulus. It is the most common loop.
    Example: High Thyroxine levels inhibit TSH secretion.
  • Positive Feedback: The end product increases the original stimulus. Used in emergency or specific short-term situations.
    Examples: Blood clotting cascade (Prothrombin activator), Uterine contractions during labor (Oxytocin), Milk ejection reflex.

2. Body Fluids

Total Body Water (TBW)

  • Water constitutes 45% to 75% of body weight.
  • Male Adults: ~60% Fluids, 40% Solids.
  • Female Adults: ~55% Fluids (less water due to higher subcutaneous adipose tissue).
  • Special Cases: Fetuses have the highest water content (95%). Old age has decreased water content due to increased adipose tissue.
  • Water Balance: Water Gain (~2500 ml/day) must equal Water Loss (~2500 ml/day).

Fluid Compartments (ICF vs ECF)

Feature Intracellular Fluid (ICF) Extracellular Fluid (ECF)
Definition & Volume Inside cells. ~25L (2/3 of TBW). Outside cells. ~17L (1/3 of TBW).
Main Cation (+) Potassium (K+) Sodium (Na+)
Main Anion (-) Phosphates & Proteins Chloride (Cl-)
pH Level 7.0 7.4

Anatomy: Introduction & Skeletal System

Foundation of Human Anatomy and Bone Structure.

1. Anatomical Position & Terms

The Anatomical Position

  • The body is standing erect.
  • The upper limbs are at the sides.
  • The face and the palms of the hands are facing forwards.
  • The feet are parallel and close together.
  • Supine position: Lying down with face directed upwards.
  • Prone position: Lying down with back upwards, face downwards.

Anatomical Planes

  • Median (Sagittal) Plane: Divides the body into equal right and left halves.
  • Parasagittal Plane: Divides the body into unequal right and left parts.
  • Coronal (Frontal) Plane: Divides the body into anterior (front) and posterior (back) parts.
  • Transverse (Horizontal) Plane: Divides the body into superior (upper) and inferior (lower) parts.

Directions & Movements

  • Proximal vs Distal: Near the trunk vs Away from the trunk.
  • Medial vs Lateral: Close to midline vs Away from midline.
  • Flexion vs Extension: Bending a joint vs Straightening a joint (Occurs around transverse axis).
  • Pronation vs Supination: Medial rotation of forearm vs Lateral rotation of forearm.
  • Inversion vs Eversion: Sole of foot faces medially vs Laterally.

2. The Skeletal System

Skeletal Divisions (206 Bones)

  • Axial Skeleton: Skull (22 bones), Thoracic cage (Sternum + 12 pairs of Ribs), Vertebral column (33 vertebrae: 7 Cervical, 12 Thoracic, 5 Lumbar, 5 Sacral, 4 Coccygeal).
  • Appendicular Skeleton: Pectoral girdle (Clavicle, Scapula), Upper limbs, Pelvic girdle (Hip bones), Lower limbs.
  • Skull details: Cranium (8 bones) + Facial skeleton (14 bones). The skull without the mandible is called the Cranium. Newborn skulls contain Fontanelles (Anterior closes at 18-24m, Posterior at 3-6m).

Bone Classification

  • By Shape: Long (Femur, Humerus), Short (Carpals), Flat (Scapula, Sternum, Ribs), Irregular (Vertebrae), Sesamoid (Patella - embedded in tendons), Pneumatic (Maxillary, Frontal).
  • By Structure: Compact bone (Hard outer layer/cortex), Spongy/Cancellous bone (Inside bones, ends of long bones).
  • Long Bone Parts: Epiphysis (ends, red marrow), Diaphysis (shaft, yellow marrow, surrounded by periosteum), Epiphyseal plate (responsible for growth in length). Main blood supply is the Nutrient Artery.
